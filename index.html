00 / Statement Artist Statement

A curve, when it is scattered into particles, becomes a constellation — harmonic, precise, quietly yours.

I am an Iranian-born artist working at the intersection of classical ornament and generative systems. My work begins with a simple observation: the parametric curve — summed from layered sines and cosines — has always carried the structure of ornament. From the Fourier series that decomposes any periodic motion into pure tones, to the geometric curves of a Persian garden plan, harmony is one of the oldest systems of mathematical beauty we have.

Fourier 2 is a seed-based generative system that translates that harmony into code. Each composition is layered in three passes: a tree of filled circles clipped inside a full-frame circle, and two separate parametric curves — each drawn not as a continuous line but as a scatter of tiny filled particles, one per sample along the curve. The result is a dense, luminous field of dots that reads as both astronomy and confetti.

The palette, the tree structure, the curve frequencies, and the particle sizes are all derived from a single numeric seed. Each piece can be regenerated, documented, and adapted for textile, print, and surface applications — from scarves and yardage to wallpaper and archival print.

On AI tools — where I encountered technical obstacles, I turned to AI tools for debugging and code optimization. Every structural, aesthetic, and conceptual decision remained my own.

03 / Process

Structure
behind the scatter.

01 / Seed

Variation

A numeric seed initializes the palette, the tree depth, the curve frequencies, the sample count, and the particle sizes. Each composition is regenerated and documented as a distinct, traceable output.

02 / Scatter

Geometry

Two parametric curves traced from summed sines and cosines, then sampled along their length. At every sample point, a small filled particle is drawn instead of a line — turning the curve into a scatter of glowing dots.

03 / Surface

Application

The resulting composition becomes a surface-design asset — explored across garments, textile prints, wallpaper and other pattern-driven applications.
